## Elevation

This is the page that explains why the semantic layer exists.

On a near-black ground, a shadow is invisible — there is nothing darker for it to
be. So in dark mode **elevation is surface lightness plus a hairline border**. In
light mode the surfaces are all white and **elevation is a shadow**. Same three
names, two completely different implementations, and a component that says
`bg-surface-2 shadow-e2` gets the right one without knowing which mode it is in.

<Rule>

  **Elevation N is `bg-surface-N` + `border border-border` + `shadow-eN`** —
  all three, always. The border is what carries the step in dark mode and the
  shadow is what carries it in light, so dropping either one breaks exactly one
  mode and you will not notice until someone flips the toggle.

  **Nesting steps the surface up one level and the radius down one step.** A card
  at `surface-1 rounded-lg` holds a row at `surface-2 rounded-md`, which holds a
  chip at `surface-3 rounded-sm`. Concentric corners want to get tighter as they
  get smaller; keeping the radius constant is what makes nested boxes look glued
  together.

</Rule>

### The same three levels, in both modes

<TwoUp render={() => <ElevationStack />} />

Look at the dark panel and then the light one. In dark, the three boxes separate
by getting **lighter**. In light, they are all <code>{value("--color-surface-1", "light")}</code> and separate by getting
**lifted**. Neither panel knows which trick it is using.
